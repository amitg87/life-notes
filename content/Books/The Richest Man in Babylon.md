## Lessons

### 7 Cures to Lean Purse

The simple rules of money acquisition

1. Start thy purse to fattening
A PART OF ALL YOU EARN IS YOURS TO KEEP.
2. Control thy expenditures
Budget your expenses - differentiate necessities and desires.
All men are burdened with more desires than they can gratify. Limit your desires - there are limits to my time, strength, distance I may travel, food I eat, luxuries I enjoy.  Delay desires - when you have excess in future.
Budgeting by accounting or separate purse system.
3. Make thy gold multiply
Magic of compounding
To put each coin to laborings that it may reproduce its kind even as the flocks of the field and help bring to thee income, a stream of wealth that shall flow constantly into thy purse. 
4. Guard thy treasures from loss
Risk assessment - The first principle of investment is security of principal. Consult with wise men - experienced in handling money for profit. Start small, gauge risk.
5. Make of thy dwelling a profitable investment
Own thy own home - reduce his cost of living, making available more of his earnings for pleasures and the gratification of his desires.
6. Insure a future income
Prepare for growing age and create protection for family. *Make preparations for a suitable income in the days to come, when he is no longer young, and to make preparations for his family should he be no longer with them to comfort and support them.*
Regular cash-flow from rental income from assets, insurance for life and disability.
7. Increase thy ability to earn
Preceding accomplishment must be desire. Thy desires must be strong and definite. Desires must be simple and definite. They defeat their own purpose should they be too many, too confusing, or beyond a man’s training to accomplish. Cultivate thy own powers, to study and become wiser, to become more skilful. Seek to learn the methods and tools of those most skilful in the same line.

### THE FIVE LAWS OF GOLD 

1. Gold cometh gladly and in increasing quantity to any man who will put by not less than one-tenth of his earnings to create an estate for his future and that of his family.
2. Gold laboreth diligently and contentedly for the wise owner who finds for it profitable employment, multiplying even as the flocks of the field. 
3. Gold clingeth to the protection of the cautious owner who invests it under the advice of men wise in its handling. 
4. Gold slippeth away from the man who invests it in businesses or purposes with which he is not familiar or which are not approved by those skilled in its keep. 
5. Gold flees the man who would force it to impossible earnings or who followeth the alluring advice of tricksters and schemers or who trusts it to his own inexperience and romantic desires in investment.

### An Historical Sketch of Babylon

Sumerians civilisation - Babylon city - wealthiest city of the ancient world - citizens . Babylon is an outstanding example of man’s ability to achieve great objectives, using whatever means are at his disposal. Babylon was located beside the Euphrates River(near modern day Baghdad, Iraq) in a flat, arid valley. Through dams and irrigation canals, residents grew abundant crops in fertile land. All of the resources supporting this large city were man-developed. All of its riches were man-made. 

Wrote things on clay tablets, which give glimpse into life millennia ago. Documented royal decrees, history, poetry, titles to property, astronomy, walled city, mathematician, etc.

### Chapter 2 - The Richest Man In Babylon

‘Fickle Fate’ is a vicious goddess who brings no permanent good to anyone. She brings ruins to almost every man upon whom she showers unearned gold.

3 types of people: 

- wanton spenders - dissipate all they receive. Have overwhelming appetites and desires that they have not the ability to gratify.
- misers and hoarders - hoard their wealth, fearing to spend what they have, knowing they do not possess the ability to replace it. They fear of robbers and doom themselves to lives of emptiness and secret misery.
- wise - earn gold, add to it and continue to be happy and contented citizens.

*An old tongue loves to wag* - when asked for advice youth will receive wisdom of years. Often youth thinks old people knows only wisdom of days that are gone. But remember this, the sun that shines today is the sun that shone when your father was born, and will still be shining when your last grandchild shall pass into the darkness.

**Pay yourself first - *A PART OF ALL YOU EARN IS YOURS TO KEEP*.** We spend money on life activities - food, house, entertainment, cloth, tax, etc. You pay to everyone but yourself. You labor for others - you are slave. Live upon less than you could earn - Income > Expense. Every saving is your slave working for you.

**Build an army of golden slaves** - Every gold piece you save is a slave to work for you. Wealth, like a tree, grows from a tiny seed. The first copper you save is the seed from which your tree of wealth shall grow. The sooner you plant that seed the sooner shall the tree grow. And the more faithfully you nourish and water that tree with consistent savings, the sooner may you bask in contentment beneath its shade. Don’t eat all your fruits - some of them are seeds for future.

*Every fool must learn - why trust the knowledge for brick maker about jewels.* Counsel with wise men - Advice is one thing that is freely given away, but watch that you take only what is worth having. He who takes advice about his savings from one who is inexperienced in such matters, shall pay with his savings for proving the falsity of their opinions. Take advice from those who are competent through their own experience.

Don’t be miser - Enjoy your life while you are here. Do not overstrain or try to save too much. Provide for family and community.

Willpower is the unflinching purpose to carry out a task you set for yourself to fulfilment. I am careful not to start difficult and impractical tasks, because I love leisure.

### Chapter 5 - The Gold Lender of Babylon

Gold bringeth unto its possessor responsibility, fear lest he lose it or it be tricked away from him, a feeling of power and ability to do good. Likewise, it bringeth opportunities whereby his very good intentions may bring him into difficulties.

Gold is the merchandise of the lender of money. It is easy to lend. If it is lent unwisely then it is difficult to get back. The wise lender wishes not the risk of the undertaking but the guarantee of safe repayment. 

I am a gold lender because I own more gold than I can use in my own trade. I desire my surplus gold to labor for others and thereby earn more gold.
It applies equally to the borrower and the lender: ***BETTER A LITTLE CAUTION THAN A GREAT REGRET.***

*It is easy to lend. If it is lent unwisely then it is difficult to get back. -* Lender must be wise and judge carefully whether gold can perform a useful purpose and return to him once more; or whether it will wasted by one unable to use it wisely and leave him without his treasure, and leave the borrower with a debt he cannot repay?

If you desire to help you friend, do so in a way that will not bring thy friends burdens upon thyself.

3 types of borrowers

1. Those who own assets(property, jewels, livestock) - loan is secured by asset
2. Those who have the capacity to earn - they have income
3. Those who have neither property nor assured earning capacity

Good merchants are an asset to our city and it profits me to aid them to keep trade moving. 

- to assist those that are in trouble,
- to help those upon whom fate has laid a heavy hand
- to help those who are starting that they may progress and become valuable citizens.
